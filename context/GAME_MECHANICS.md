# Game Mechanics

## Screen Layout
- **Bottom half**: Match-3 board
- **Upper half**: Battlefield (left vs right facing)

## Battlefield Structure
- Each side has two zones — **Melee** (front line) and **Ranged** (back line) — but there is **no fixed slot or lane count**. Instead, `TEAM_CAP = 5`: a hard cap on total characters per side, any mix of Ranged and Melee (e.g. 5 Ranged/0 Melee, 2 Ranged/3 Melee, etc. are all legal).
- Missions can have uneven team sizes (example: player 4 vs enemy 5, both still ≤ `TEAM_CAP`)
- Heroes are placed by hand pre-battle from your available roster (the full 45-character roster — the enemy's team does not restrict the player's choices). A collapsible filter panel (behind the "▾ Filter" toggle button, `#btn-filter-toggle` — closed by default, opens `#deploy-filter-bar`) holds: Melee, Ranged, Heroes, Non-Heroes chips, a "🎨 Colors" second-level disclosure (its own toggle, not a filter — reveals a sub-row of 5 color chips: Red/Blue/Green/Yellow/Purple, the actual color filters), and a Clear button that stays grayed out and unclickable until at least one filter is active. Chips are independently toggleable and combine across three groups (type, hero-status, color) — multiple chips within the same group are OR'd together, while chips across groups are AND'd (e.g. Heroes + Ranged + Red narrows to red Ranged Heroes only). The enemy team is randomized fresh every time you (re)enter deployment — see "Enemy Team Randomization" below.
- **The deployment screen and the live battle screen render the player's own Ranged/Melee columns identically.** Ranged is always the fixed `RANGED_SLOTS` grid (same row positions whether you're still placing heroes or mid-fight); Melee cards stay pixel-aligned to their `band` in both screens. A hero never visually jumps to a different row/position when you hit Start Battle. (The enemy's columns are a simple centered list in both screens, since the enemy roster isn't interactively arranged by the player.)
- The dashed "placement lining" around the player's Melee column (a drag-target hint) only shows during deployment — it disappears once the battle starts, since nothing is draggable there anymore. A separate, permanent dotted divider marks the boundary between the player's two columns and the enemy's two columns in both screens (deliberately a plain "your side / their side" separator, distinct in style from the deployment-only dashed hint — noted as a placeholder ("for now") worth revisiting with more visual polish later).
- **Placing a new hero: tap the "+" badge, or drag (mouse only).** Every roster card shows a small green "+" glyph in its upper-right corner (no circle/background, just the colored glyph with a dark outline for legibility) — tapping/clicking it auto-places that hero in the next open row from the top (Ranged: first empty of the `RANGED_SLOTS` slots; Melee: first Ranged-row index not already guarded by an *aligned* Melee — see `autoPlaceCharacter()`). This is the only way to place a NEW hero on touch — dragging a roster card is mouse-only (`opts.fromSlot`/`opts.meleeIndex == null && e.pointerType !== 'mouse'` bails out of `startRangedDrag()`/`startMeleeDrag()`), so a touch pointerdown on a roster card does nothing at all and there's no conflict with scrolling the list.
- **Repositioning an already-placed hero: drag, on any input.** Once a card is on the battlefield, dragging it works the same for touch and mouse (the mouse-only restriction above only applies to *new* placements from the roster) — the battlefield doesn't scroll, so there's no touch/drag conflict to protect against there. This is how touch reaches a Melee's half-step boundary position, which "+" can't express on its own: place aligned via "+", then drag that card down into the gap between two Ranged rows. Placed cards carry `touch-action: none` — without it, a touch-and-hold on a card is ambiguous to the browser (it can read as "start selecting text" instead of "grab this card"), which both blocks the drag and leaves the card visually stuck mid-gesture. `.card` also sets `-webkit-touch-callout: none` so iOS doesn't offer its long-press selection/callout menu on a card either.
- **Every placed card shows a small red "−" glyph** in its upper-right corner (same bare-glyph style as "+") — tapping/clicking it removes that hero and returns it to the roster (`unplaceRanged()`/`unplaceMelee()`), on any input.
- **Ranged placement**: the fixed `RANGED_SLOTS` (= `TEAM_CAP` = 5) grid — filled or empty ("tap + to place" placeholder) at fixed positions. "+" always fills the first empty slot; dragging (mouse-only for a new roster card, any input to reposition an already-placed one) can drop on any specific empty slot instead — the player's choice of exact position, so gaps are allowed (e.g. slots 1 and 3 filled, others empty). Dropping on an already-occupied slot is rejected (no swap); dragging a placed card outside the battlefield removes it.
- **Melee placement**: no fixed slots of its own — every Melee card locks onto one of two positions relative to the (always-rendered, filled-or-not) Ranged slots: **level with one Ranged row**, or **on the half-step gap between two adjacent Ranged rows** (straddling both; a new card only reaches this via drag — mouse for a roster card, any input once it's already placed — since "+" only ever places aligned). Dragging a Melee character up and down live-previews this: the target row/gap lights up gold as you move, and any Ranged slot(s) it would actually protect (see below) additionally light up green. Releasing over the Melee column snaps the card into place there; releasing outside cancels the placement (or removes the hero, if it was already placed). Two Melee may never occupy visually-overlapping positions (see below) — "+" and drag both enforce this the same way.
- **No visually-overlapping Melee positions**: rejected on both "+" and drag. This is stricter than "identical band" — an aligned card and either of its two ADJACENT boundary positions overlap heavily on screen (a boundary sits at the midpoint between two row centers, and card height is a large fraction of the row pitch), so aligned-row-`i` conflicts with boundary-`(i-1,i)` and boundary-`(i,i+1)` too, not just another aligned-row-`i`. Two boundary bands that merely share one index but not the other (e.g. `{0,1}` and `{1,2}`) do NOT conflict — they sit a full row apart — this is exactly the "two Melee side by side" enemy formation pattern (see `ENEMY_FORMATIONS`). See `bandsConflict()`.
- **Drag preview**: while dragging (Ranged or Melee, from the roster on a mouse, or repositioning an already-placed card on any input), the element following the cursor/finger is a full card — same color gradient, icon, name, HP text, and special-hero glow as the placed card — not a stripped-down label. See `buildGhost()`.
- Two Melee heroes may **not** share the same row or the same half-step gap — dropping one onto a row/gap another Melee already occupies is rejected (logged, placement unchanged); drop on a different row or half-step instead. (Earlier versions allowed stacking with a small fan-out offset; this was reversed — see DECISIONS.md.)
- Each Melee card is rendered pixel-aligned with the Ranged row (or gap) it's on — not just sorted into a list — so "same row" and "half step" are literal visual facts, not an approximation. The Ranged column always shows its `RANGED_SLOTS` fixed rows (filled or empty); Melee cards align against that same grid. Each Melee card shows a coverage tag (e.g. "🛡 R1" or "🛡 R1+R2") indicating what it currently guards.

### Melee Stagger / Half-Step Coverage
A Melee hero's **position** and what it **protects** are two separate things:
- **Position (`band`)** is pure geometry — which Ranged row it's level with, or which adjacent pair of rows it straddles — and is always resolvable, since the Ranged grid (`RANGED_SLOTS`, fixed length) exists whether or not those slots are actually filled. Placing a Melee hero next to a still-empty Ranged row is allowed; it just isn't protecting anyone yet.
- **Protection (`coverage`)** is derived live from position + which Ranged slots are currently filled: a Melee **fully aligned** to one Ranged row fully covers just that lane. A Melee on a **boundary/half-step** position shares cover of both neighboring rows, but **only if both are actually filled** ("as long as both ranged are together"). If only one neighboring row has a Ranged hero, it simply guards that one; if neither does yet, it guards nothing — until a Ranged unit is later placed into one of those rows, at which point the already-placed Melee hero automatically starts protecting it (no need to re-drag).
- Coverage is **binary** — "partial" and "full" protection both fully block opposing melee from reaching that ranged unit; there is no damage-reduction gradient.
- Implementation: `computeSlotBand(rects, y)` is a pure geometry function — given the live on-screen positions of all `RANGED_SLOTS` Ranged slots (filled or empty) and a Y coordinate, it returns which row or which boundary gap that Y falls on. `resolveMeleeCoverage(band, filledMask)` turns a `band` into actual coverage (`{type:'aligned', lane}`, `{type:'boundary', lanes:[a,b]}`, or `null`) using which slots are currently filled — called fresh whenever coverage needs to be known (rendering, battle start), not frozen at drop time. There is no static slot → lane table.
- With only 5 total characters per side, most deployments will have very few Melee guarding many Ranged lanes (or vice versa) — but double-guarding one lane by stacking two Melee on the exact same row/half-step is no longer possible (see above); a boundary placement is the only way one Melee covers two lanes at once.

### Targeting Rules
- Melee units can always attack any living enemy Melee unit.
- Melee units can attack a specific enemy Ranged unit only if no living enemy Melee's `coverage` currently guards that unit's lane (see coverage section above). This is checked **per ranged unit**, not per zone — a lane can be open for attack even while other enemy Melee units are still alive elsewhere.
- Ranged units can attack any enemy unit (they shoot over the melee line).

### Formation Info Bubble & Formation Presets (deployment screen only)
Two separate, deliberately-decoupled features live behind the deployment screen's `#placement-controls` row:
- **Info bubble** (`#btn-formation-info` → `#formation-info-bubble`): a small "ℹ" button just left of the Formations button toggles a speech-bubble tooltip holding the melee-coverage explainer (the same 3 bullets the old Formation popup used to show). `positionFormationInfoBubble()` computes its position at open time — anchored just left of the info button but clamped to `left: max(8px, ...)` so it can never run off the left edge of the viewport, since the button sits close enough to the page's left margin that a plain CSS anchor would push part of the bubble off-screen on this layout. Closes on a second tap of the info button or a tap anywhere else on the page (a document-level click listener checks `!bubble.contains(e.target)`).
- **Formation Presets** (`#btn-formation` → `#formation-modal`): opens a swipeable carousel of `FORMATION_PRESETS` — six ready-made Ranged-row/Melee-band shapes, each demonstrating a different practical use of aligned vs. boundary (staggered) coverage. A preset is `{ name, blurb, ranged: [rowIndex, ...], melee: [{type:'aligned', index} | {type:'boundary', indices:[i, i+1]}, ...] }`, using real `RANGED_SLOTS` row indices (0..4) — unlike `ENEMY_FORMATIONS` above (which uses simple 0-based "lanes" since the enemy renders as a plain list, not a pixel-aligned grid), every preset's melee bands are pre-verified pairwise `bandsConflict()`-free so they render without visual overlap, the same guarantee manual placement enforces.
  - Each slide is built from that data by `buildFormationSlideEl()`/`buildFormationPreviewSVG()` — a title, a one-line tactical blurb, and a mini diagram (RANGED_SLOTS rows stacked vertically, filled = 🏹 box / empty = faint dashed outline, melee bands drawn beside them level with an aligned row or spanning the half-step between two rows for a boundary, plus a single "ENEMY →" arrow). The carousel mechanics (swipe via pointerdown/up threshold, ‹›, dots) are unchanged from the old explainer, just re-pointed at `FORMATION_PRESETS.length` (6) slides instead of 3 hardcoded ones.
  - **Applying a preset** (`applyFormationPreset()`) clears the current deployment, then reassigns it: it prefers reusing whichever specific heroes the player had already placed (any position, either type — tracked before the clear) to fill the preset's Ranged rows/Melee bands, and only pulls a fresh hero from the roster for a slot when there aren't enough already-chosen heroes of the needed type left. If the preset needs *fewer* of a type than the player had deployed, the extras simply return to the roster. This means applying a preset reorganizes an existing pick into an efficient shape rather than discarding it, while still guaranteeing every slot gets filled.
  - The six presets: **Twin Wall** (3 Ranged/2 Melee, two boundary guards side-by-side — max lane coverage per melee), **Spearhead** (1 Ranged/4 Melee, one protected unit behind a wall of blades), **Checkerboard** (3 Ranged/2 Melee, spread lanes with one flank deliberately exposed), **Bottleneck** (4 Ranged/1 Melee, a wide line funneled through one boundary choke point), **Vanguard Brawl** (2 Ranged/3 Melee, one boundary guard plus two forward aligned melee with no ranged behind them, for extra frontline damage/soak), **Solo Guard** (1 Ranged/1 Melee, the minimal aligned pair — deliberately smaller than `TEAM_CAP` as a lean starting option).

## Enemy Team Randomization
- The enemy roster is regenerated every time deployment is (re)entered (`randomizeEnemyTeam()`, called from `initDeployment()`) — not fixed per mission. Always `TEAM_CAP` (5) total.
- **Formation (Ranged/Melee split)**: one of two `ENEMY_FORMATIONS` is picked with equal probability on every draw:
  - **3 Melee / 2 Ranged** — the original static team's lane pattern: one Melee aligned to lane 0, one straddling the boundary between lanes 0/1, one aligned to lane 1.
  - **2 Melee / 3 Ranged** — two Melee side by side, each on a boundary: one straddling lanes 0/1, one straddling lanes 1/2. Together they cover all 3 lanes (lane 1 doubly, by both) with only 2 Melee.
  - Both shapes always total `TEAM_CAP` (5); which one is drawn only affects the Ranged/Melee split and the Melee coverage pattern, not the hero-cap or color-diversity rules below.
- **Hero cap**: at most 2 of the 5 may be Heroes (`isSpecial`) — at least 3 must be plain non-hero troops. Enforced by construction: the draw first decides how many Heroes (0, 1, or 2) and how they split across the Ranged/Melee roles, then picks specific characters from the matching type+specialty pool — it does not rely on rejecting random 5-character draws until one happens to satisfy the cap (with roughly 7 of every 10 characters being Heroes, that would rarely succeed quickly).
- **Color diversity**: the 5 chosen characters must show at least 4 distinct colors (of the 5 possible match-3 colors — so at most one same-color pair is allowed). Enforced by retrying the draw (bounded attempts) until satisfied; much higher base probability than the hero constraint, so this converges quickly in practice.
- The player's own character pool is unaffected — any of the 45 characters may still be picked regardless of what the randomizer drew for the enemy, including the same character on both sides.
- **Hidden during deployment**: the deployment (character placement) screen shows no enemy section at all — not even a placeholder column. Only the player's own Ranged/Melee columns are rendered, so neither the enemy's characters nor which formation was drawn (and therefore not even the Ranged/Melee count split) leak before the player commits their own team. `ENEMY_TEAM` is decided once, at deployment-entry time, same as before — only the on-screen reveal is deferred to `renderBattlefield()` (called from `startBattle()`), which shows the real roster (both sides, with their "YOU"/"ENEMY" labels) exactly as it always has.
- **Placement screen layout**: with no enemy section, `renderDeployBattlefield()` adds a `deploy-battlefield-narrow` class to `#battlefield` that halves its max-width (420px → 220px), so the two remaining columns' cards stay close to their original size instead of stretching to fill the row — this also happens to keep their per-card width close to what the 4-column battle screen renders, preserving the spirit of CLAUDE.md's "layout must stay visually identical" rule even though the enemy side differs. `renderBattlefield()` removes the class again so it never leaks into the live battle screen. The column labels here are bare icons (`🏹`/`⚔️`, no "YOU" text) — the live battle screen's labels are unchanged ("YOU 🏹"/"YOU ⚔️"/"ENEMY ⚔️"/"ENEMY 🏹"). The Melee column's outer dashed "dropzone" outline is gone too (`.bf-column.dropzone-hint` no longer draws one) — only each individual empty-slot card's own dashed border remains; the `.dropzone-hint` class itself is still applied, now purely to scope the CSS rule that stretches the empty-melee placeholder to fill the column's height.
- **Placement screen controls**: two static, centered rows directly underneath the placement box. Row 1 (`#placement-controls`): Formation button → "Deployed: N/5" status text (`#deploy-status`) → Character Codex link (`#btn-character-codex`, now deployment-only — it used to be a permanent top-of-page link on both screens). Row 2 (`#filter-toggle-wrap`): the Filter toggle button alone, centered. Both rows are shown/hidden the same way `#end-turn-wrap` is (`initDeployment()` shows them, `startBattle()` hides them), so all three of Formation/Codex/Filter are deployment-only and never appear on the live battle screen — same for `#deploy-filter-bar`, which sits directly below row 2 once expanded (right under the button that opens it). `#btn-formation` deliberately sits in its row rather than overlaid on a corner of the box, so it can never overlap a placed card regardless of team composition. Tapping it opens `#formation-modal`: a static 3-line text block, plus a 3-slide swipeable carousel of inline-SVG diagrams below it (one per line — Melee blocking, Ranged safe behind Melee, one Melee straddling two Ranged). The carousel (`formationIndex`, `updateFormationTrack()`) responds to pointer-drag swipe (works for touch/mouse/pen), tappable dot indicators, and ‹/› buttons alike. No image assets exist anywhere in this project — the "photos" are built entirely from inline SVG using the same emoji icons and color palette as the rest of the UI.

## Match-3 Board
- Each hero has their **own individual mana/stamina bar**.
- Matches never create new soldier/hero units on the battlefield. As of the Match Type Rules overhaul below, matches of 4+ tiles CAN deal direct damage to the enemy team — this supersedes the older "Match-3 never deals direct damage" rule.
- `board[y][x]` holds each cell's color; a parallel grid `boardSpecial[y][x]` (null | `'bomb'` | `'crystal'`) tracks whether that cell also holds a special tile. A cell's color and its special status move together through gravity/fill (`applyGravityAndFill()`).
- **Input: tap-and-hold, then drag/swipe onto a neighboring tile** (up/down/left/right) to swap — the standard match-3 gesture, not a select-then-select-again tap flow. `onTilePointerDown()` starts tracking a press; `onTilePointerMove()` fires the swap (`attemptTileSwap()`) the instant the drag crosses `TILE_SWIPE_THRESHOLD_PX` (16px) in a clear dominant direction — no need to release first, and only one swap can happen per press-drag gesture. A plain tap with no drag does nothing on an ordinary tile; the two special tiles (Arcane Bomb, Runic Crystal) are the exception, both activating immediately on tap regardless of drag distance (`activateSpecialTap()`, on `onTilePointerUp()` when no drag occurred). Tiles show no letter/text (removed per direct request) — only `boardSpecial` tiles (💣/🔮) show anything at all.
- **Swaps slide visually instead of teleporting.** `animateTileSwap(x1,y1,x2,y2,onDone)` is a FLIP animation: it swaps the two cells' data in `board`/`boardSpecial` first, calls `renderBoard()`, then reads each tile element's now-wrong-looking `getBoundingClientRect()`, applies an inverse `transform:translate()` so it visually appears back at its old position, forces a reflow (`void el.offsetWidth`), and on the next frame animates `transform` back to `translate(0,0)` over `SWAP_SLIDE_MS` (160ms). `attemptTileSwap()` calls this, waits for `onDone`, then runs match detection; if the swap produced no match and activated no bomb, the same slide plays once more in reverse to visually undo it (board data is swapped back first, same as the forward case). `isAnimating`/board-lock are now set at the very start of `attemptTileSwap()` rather than only once a match is confirmed, since the slide introduces a real async window before the outcome is known.
- **Cleared tiles spawn glittering mana shard particles that fly to the matching hero's mana bar.** `spawnManaShards(x,y,color)` is called once per cell from the single commit point in `processMatches()` where all cleared cells (plain matches, bomb-blast neighbors, crystal full-color clears) get nulled out — so it fires uniformly across every clear path. For every living player hero whose `color` matches the cleared tile, it spawns 3 small diamond `.mana-shard` divs at the tile's screen position with slight random jitter and staggers their departure ~45ms apart. The actual spawn/stagger/fade/cleanup loop lives in a shared `flyManaShards(startX,startY,endX,endY,hex,count)` helper (in a dedicated fixed-position `#shard-layer` overlay, `z-index:500`) — reused as-is by the enemy's off-screen mana shards (see Enemy Turn below) with only the start point and color differing. Each shard animates its `left`/`top` toward the center of the target hero's `.mana-bar-bg` while fading/shrinking, removing itself once the flight (~550ms including stagger) completes. Purely cosmetic: it runs independently of and has no effect on the actual mana math, which `grantMana()` still computes separately from the same cleared-cell data.

### Match Type Rules
Match resolution groups matched cells into same-color, orthogonally-connected components (`findMatchGroups()`) rather than a flat per-color tally — this is what makes an intersecting horizontal-3 + vertical-3 register as one 5-cell T/L/plus-shaped group instead of two separate 3-groups. Every tier grants **1 mana per tile, uniformly** — there is no extra flat bonus for larger matches; the only differentiation between tiers is which special tile spawns.

- **Match of 3 tiles** — removes the tiles. Grants 1 mana per tile to every living player hero of that color. No other effect.
- **Match of 4 tiles → Arcane Bomb** — grants 1 mana per tile immediately, and one cell of the group becomes an **Arcane Bomb** (`boardSpecial = 'bomb'`) instead of clearing (chosen by `pickSpawnCell()`: the cell the player's swap landed on, if part of the group, else the group's shape "intersection" cell, else the middle of a straight run). When the Arcane Bomb is later **matched** (caught in a new same-color connected match) **or activated** (the player swaps any adjacent tile into or out of its cell, even if that swap doesn't itself form a new color-match):
  - It removes itself + its 4 orthogonal neighbors (up/down/left/right).
  - All 5 tiles (bomb cell + 4 neighbors) pay out mana as normal, each by **its own** color.
  - It deals 2 damage to one random living enemy Melee (front-line) unit. If no enemy Melee is alive, this just fizzles (logged, no damage).
  - If a neighbor cell itself holds a pre-existing special tile, that tile is queued to explode too (chain reaction) before the wave is considered fully resolved.
- **Match of 5+ tiles (a straight run of 5, or a T/L/plus shape formed by two intersecting runs of ≥3) → Runic Crystal** — grants 1 mana per tile immediately, and one cell becomes a **Runic Crystal** (`boardSpecial = 'crystal'`, spawn cell chosen the same way as the bomb). When the Runic Crystal is later matched (caught in a fresh same-color connected match) or activated (a direct tap):
  - Its own tile always clears and pays its base 1 mana first, regardless of trigger path.
  - The player then chooses one of two effects via a modal (`showCrystalChoiceModal()`):
    - **Option A — Clear Color**: clears every remaining tile of that color anywhere on the board, granting mana for all of them (big mana gain). Any pre-existing special caught in this clear is queued to explode too (chain reaction).
    - **Option B — Runic Wave**: no mana; instead releases a simultaneous wave of damage down all `RANGED_SLOTS` (= 5) rows/lanes at once. See "Runic Wave Targeting" below for the per-lane math.

### Special Tile Activation & Chain Reactions
Arcane Bomb and Runic Crystal share two triggers — "matched into a new group" and a direct tap — but the Bomb has one extra trigger the Crystal doesn't (a swap that merely touches its cell):
- **Arcane Bomb**: "matched or activated" means caught in a fresh same-color connected match (`findMatchGroups()`) — i.e. the player swaps in at least two more same-color tiles that connect to it, forming a new ≥3 group that includes its cell — **or** the player swaps (drags) any adjacent tile touching its cell (`attemptTileSwap()`), even when that swap creates no new color-match at all, since that's what makes a deliberate "detonate this bomb" move possible — **or** the player directly taps its tile (`activateSpecialTap()`, fired from `onTilePointerUp()` when the press ended without a drag) — a faster way to set it off without needing an adjacent tile to swap with. Unlike a Crystal, a Bomb can still be dragged as part of a normal swap (which itself also detonates it, per the trigger above) — tapping is simply additive, not exclusive. Any of the three consumes the turn's one board action.
- **Runic Crystal**: activates the same "matched into a new group" way a Bomb does (same-color connected match reaching its cell), **or** via a **direct tap** on its own tile (`activateSpecialTap()`, same function the Bomb's tap uses). Either path opens the same choice modal. A crystal merely touched by an unrelated swap between two other tiles, with no color-connection formed through its cell, still just clears like an ordinary matched tile (its group-share mana still pays out) with no special effect and no modal. A crystal's own cell can never be one half of a normal two-tile swap — dragging a neighbor onto or off of one is a no-op (`attemptTileSwap()`'s crystal guard); only the direct tap activates it. Either trigger consumes the turn's one board action, same as a swap, and is blocked (with a log message) if that action was already used this turn.

Both trigger paths converge on the same explosion machinery (`processExplosionQueue()` — a continuation-passing walk, needed because the Runic Crystal's player-choice modal has to pause mid-resolution, and because a bomb/crystal's own effect can catch another pre-existing special and grow the queue mid-walk).

### Runic Wave Targeting (`resolveRunicWaveHits()`)
A Runic Wave travels down all `RANGED_SLOTS` rows/lanes simultaneously and, in each lane, hits only the first living enemy unit it meets:
- **No guarding Melee in that lane** → the enemy Ranged unit there (if any) takes the full 3 damage.
- **A Melee fully aligned to that lane** (`coverage: {type:'aligned', lane}`) → the Melee takes the full 3 damage; the Ranged unit behind it is **completely protected** (0 damage, not even a token hit).
- **A Melee straddling that lane and the next** (`coverage: {type:'boundary', lanes:[a,b]}`) → the Melee still takes the full 3 damage, but only **once** (not once per lane it straddles). Of its two guarded Ranged lanes, one stays fully protected and the other is "half exposed" and takes `ceil(3/2) = 2` damage. The data model has no inherent left/right lean for a straddling Melee, so which of its two lanes is the exposed one is chosen at random **per activation** (see DECISIONS.md).

## Hero Actions
- **Basic Attack**: Low mana/stamina cost. Always available.
- **Abilities**: Unlocked through progression. Higher mana/stamina cost. More powerful effects or utility.
- Heroes can be leveled and developed to gain new abilities.
- A player turn always includes exactly one Match-3 swap; after that, each living player hero may act once (a normal character's only option is Basic; a Hero (`isSpecial`) additionally gets a "Do Nothing" choice in its action popup, to bank mana instead of attacking). The turn auto-advances to the enemy once every living hero has either acted or can't afford its own Basic cost.
- **Manual attack input**: `onHeroClick()`/`resolveAttack()`. A player hero that can currently act shows a gold pulsing glow (`.card.can-act`, driven by `canHeroAct()`). This must be recomputed for every living hero, not just whichever ones the preceding action directly touched — `checkAutoEndPlayerTurn()` (called after every swap, attack, and Do Nothing) runs `heroes.forEach(updateHeroUI)` for exactly this reason, since e.g. `grantMana()` only calls `updateHeroUI()` on heroes of the matched color, which would otherwise leave every other hero's glow stale after a swap that didn't touch their color. Tapping a glowing hero attacks right away — a normal character straight to Basic, a Hero after its popup's Basic/Special choice (Do Nothing still just banks, unchanged). Tapping an enemy never itself attacks; it only sets an optional pre-aim (`aimedTarget`) — shown with the same green glow `.card.selected` already used elsewhere (repurposed: it no longer means "a player hero is mid-selection", since that state doesn't exist anymore — attacks resolve in one hop). Tapping the already-aimed enemy again clears it. Aim is consumed by the very next attack that resolves (successful or not, valid or falling back) regardless of which hero/path triggered it — there's no "sticky" multi-attack aim.
  - **Logical Targeting** (`pickLogicalTarget()`) is what a hero attacks when there's no valid pre-aim: it ranks `getValidTargets(attacker)` — never anything geometric/pixel-based, exactly the same legal-target list the melee/ranged lane rules already produce — by (1) highest current mana (same flat `SPECIAL_COST` for everyone, so this is directly "closest to affording Special"), (2) then lowest HP, (3) then nearest lane to the attacker's own via `heroLane()` (unifies Ranged's `laneIndex` and Melee's `primaryLane(coverage)`, since Melee has no lane of its own).
  - If the player pre-aimed at a target that turns out illegal for the specific hero they then tap (e.g. aimed at a Ranged unit a Melee attacker can't reach), `resolveAttack()` silently falls back to the Logical Target rather than blocking the attack or erroring.
  - This is entirely separate from the Auto Attack toggle below, which still always picks a uniformly random valid target — Auto Attack was intentionally left as "semi-logical" (see its own entry), not upgraded to Logical Targeting.
- **Full-mana glow**: a Hero's (`isSpecial`) mana bar gets its own gold pulse (`.mana-bar.mana-full`, toggled in `updateHeroUI()`) once `mana >= maxMana` — a separate visual signal from `.card.can-act`, specifically "this Hero is fully charged," so the player can spot who's worth using Special on without reading numbers. Normal characters never get this, regardless of how full their bar is — they have no Special to build toward.
- **HP display (live battle only)**: a hero's HP is shown as a depleting bar (`.hp-bar-bg`/`.hp-bar`, red gradient fill sized to `hp/maxHp`, CSS `transition:width` for a smooth drain) with the numeric `hp/maxHp` ratio still overlaid on top as text (`.hp-num`) — built in `buildHeroCardEl()`, kept in sync in `updateHeroUI()` on every HP change. This is scoped to the live battle screen only; the deployment screen's preview card (`buildDeployPreviewCard`) still shows plain `N HP` text since it only ever displays full, undamaged HP (no bar needed there).
- **End Turn button**: sits between the Battlefield and the Match-3 board (`#end-turn-wrap`), visible only during battle. Lets the player skip straight to the enemy turn in one tap — banking mana for every Hero that hasn't acted yet — instead of opening each Hero's popup individually to pick "Do Nothing". Requires the turn's swap first (same as attacking), **and** is blocked while any normal (non-`isSpecial`) character can still afford its Basic Attack and hasn't acted — normal characters have no Do Nothing option and must attack if able, so End Turn can't be used to bypass that. The button is visibly grayed out (`opacity: 0.4`, `disabled`) whenever it's unusable, not just click-blocked — kept in sync via `updateEndTurnButtonState()`, called from `updateHeroUI()`/`updateTurnUI()`/`checkAutoEndPlayerTurn()`.

## Enemy Turn
- `endPlayerTurn()` locks the board and flips `currentTurn` to `'enemy'` (so the "Enemy Turn..." indicator shows immediately), then waits `ENEMY_TURN_START_DELAY_MS` (900ms) before actually calling `enemyTurn()` — a deliberate pause so the turn handoff reads as a real beat rather than the enemy instantly acting the moment the indicator changes.
- `enemyTurn()` first boosts 1-2 random living enemies' mana by a random amount (`3 + random(0-2)`), simulating "their match" since the enemy has no visible board of their own. Each boosted hero's mana grant is now accompanied by `spawnEnemyManaShards(hero)` — shard particles that fall from just off-screen above the viewport (`top: -20px`) down into that hero's mana bar, dramatizing the gain the same way a player's tile match spawns shards flying to their mana bar (see `spawnManaShards`/`flyManaShards` under Match-3 Board). The off-screen-above origin is deliberate: it reads as "the enemy's own match-3 board is hidden just above the screen," giving their mana gain a visual source without needing to actually render one.
- Each enemy that can afford Basic then attacks a random valid target one at a time, staggered via increasing `setTimeout` delays (500ms apart) so attacks resolve visibly rather than all at once; the first attack's delay was pushed from 400ms to 700ms so it starts after the mana shards' flight (~590ms including their spawn stagger) has visibly landed, rather than attacking before the player can see where the mana went. A Hero (`isSpecial`) that can afford Special always uses it over Basic.

Side is still fully legible from position alone (player columns left of the dotted `.side-divider`, enemy columns right of it) plus the new enemy-facing-left mirroring described next.

This is a pure CSS horizontal flip applied to the sprite element itself — it has no art-asset cost (no separate mirrored Idle/Attack/Impacted images needed) and applies uniformly across all three poses automatically, since `triggerHeroPose()` only ever swaps which image URL fills the same already-mirrored `.hero-sprite` box.

Fixed with a deliberately-more-specific `.card.sprite-style[data-color] { background: none; }` (matching `[data-color]` with no required value still matches every real card) — see DECISIONS.md. Worth remembering for any future `.card` styling: these color-tint rules sit late in the file and will re-win any same-specificity override placed earlier, regardless of how clearly that override's intent reads in isolation.

Both `buildHeroCardEl()` (Idle, at build time) and `triggerHeroPose()` (Attack/Impacted, at trigger time) set it via the DOM property on `.hero-sprite` instead, which has no such quoting hazard. An earlier attempt at showing the art at all — an `<img class="card-art">` child positioned with `z-index:-1` to sit behind the card's text — hit a *different*, now-moot bug (`.card` used `position:relative` with no explicit `z-index`, so it never actually formed a stacking context, and the negative z-index silently escaped past the card and painted behind unrelated ancestors); that approach was replaced by a background-image on the card before this round even started, and this round just moved that background-image from the card itself onto the new `.hero-sprite` child. See DECISIONS.md for both debugging stories.
  - `triggerHeroPose(hero, pose)` briefly swaps `.hero-sprite`'s background to its `'attack'` or `'impacted'` pose, then reverts to `'idle'` after `HERO_POSE_DURATION_MS` (550ms) — a fire-and-forget visual flourish that never gates or delays game logic, the same spirit as the mana shard particles. Hooked into `performAttack()` (attacker → `'attack'`, target → `'impacted'`, right where mana/HP are actually debited) and `dealBoardDamage()` (target → `'impacted'`, covering Arcane Bomb and Runic Wave damage too). Any pending revert timer is cleared before starting a new one, so a hero hit again mid-flash doesn't snap back to Idle under a newer pose.
    - **Real bug fixed per direct report**: "when characters change poses for any reason, they fade or are less visible." Root cause was a *different*, coincidentally-timed mechanism, not the pose swap itself — `performAttack()` sets `attacker.actedThisTurn = true` immediately after `triggerHeroPose(attacker, 'attack')`, and `updateHeroUI()`'s old `.card.acted { opacity:0.5; filter:grayscale(0.7); }` rule then dimmed the attacker's whole card for the rest of the turn, starting right as its flashy Attack pose began playing — so every attack looked like "the pose triggers a fade." Fixed by deleting the `.card.acted` CSS rule and the `classList.toggle('acted', ...)` call in `updateHeroUI()` that fed it; `actedThisTurn` remains fully intact as game-logic state (still read directly off the hero object by `canHeroAct()`, `.can-act`'s glow, `normalCharacterMustActFirst()`, and End Turn's gating) — only the redundant visual dimming signal was removed. `.card.dead`'s opacity/grayscale is unchanged and intentionally kept, since "defeated" is a meaningfully different and more permanent state than "already acted this turn."
  - An unfilled Ranged slot during battle (`buildEmptySpriteSlot()`) is built from the exact same pieces (header row, square sprite, HP/mana bars) as a real card, just left invisible (`.battle-empty-slot`) — this matters functionally, not just visually: Melee band alignment measures every Ranged row's live rect, filled or not, so a mismatched empty-row height would throw off where a boundary card centers itself.

## Battle Screen Fit & Controls
Per direct request, the whole battle screen (scenery + battlefield + controls + Match-3 board) is visible on a phone at first glance, without scrolling. The current implementation is a **fixed viewport-percentage grid**, not natural content-flow sizing — this supersedes an earlier round's approach of trimming individual element sizes (fixed px reductions to `.hero-sprite`'s height, board tile size, paddings/margins) to make content-driven height happen to fit; that approach still let a large enough team push the page past one screen on a smaller device, since nothing was actually fixed to the viewport.
- **`#battle-scene.battle-active { display:grid; grid-template-rows: 9% 50% auto 1fr 4%; height:100dvh; }`** — toggled on by `startBattle()`/`restartBattle()`, off by `initDeployment()`. Percentages in `grid-template-rows` resolve against the grid container's own height; `1fr` automatically absorbs "the remainder" (Match-3) after every fixed/percentage/`auto` row is subtracted, so no manual math is needed to make Match-3 "take up the remainder." `100dvh` (dynamic viewport height) is used instead of `100vh` because `100vh` can be taller than what's actually visible while a mobile browser's address bar hasn't yet collapsed — sizing to `100vh` here would risk a phantom scrollbar/overflow that `100dvh` avoids. Deployment never gets `.battle-active`, so its natural-flow, scrollable layout is untouched by any of this.
- **Five visible rows, five visible children, in DOM order**: `#scenery-band`, `#battlefield`, `#end-turn-wrap`, `#board-wrap`, `#bottom-buffer`. `#scenery-band` and `#bottom-buffer` are new, content-free divs (`display:none` outside `.battle-active`) that exist purely to claim their grid row — the shared map background image (already set on `#battle-scene` itself, see the "underground Match-3" background below) shows through both since neither has a fill of its own, so the scenery band reads as "more of the same backdrop" above the battlefield, and the bottom buffer as reserved empty space (a few % kept clear at the very bottom for phone case bezels/notches/home indicators) rather than a solid color block. Every other direct child of `#battle-scene` (`#placement-controls`, `#filter-toggle-wrap`, `#deploy-filter-bar`, `#deploy-panel`) is deployment-only and already `display:none` by the time `.battle-active` is applied, so CSS Grid's auto-placement skips them — the 5-row template only ever has to account for the 5 elements actually visible during battle.
- **`#turn-indicator` lives inside `#end-turn-wrap`** (as its first child, before `#battle-controls-row`), not as a standalone sibling after `#battle-scene` like before. It already shared `#end-turn-wrap`'s exact show/hide toggle timing, so nesting it changed nothing functionally, but it now rides inside the `auto` controls row instead of adding a 6th row's worth of height below the whole 100dvh grid — a standalone sibling there would have pushed the page taller than one screen regardless of how tightly the 5 named rows were sized. **Per direct follow-up request, this element's text ("Your Turn — swap two tiles, then attack" / "Enemy Turn..." / "Battle Over") is no longer shown at all** — `startBattle()` simply no longer flips its `display` to `'block'`, so it stays at its CSS default `display:none` permanently. `updateTurnUI()` still updates its `textContent`/color on every turn change exactly as before (harmless on a hidden element) rather than being special-cased to skip that work, and the element itself stays in the DOM (same treatment as `.info`/`#log` elsewhere in this file) since `updateTurnUI()`'s lookup has no null guard removal path. The `auto` controls row simply sizes itself to whatever's left (`#battle-controls-row` alone) now that the indicator contributes no height.
- **`body.battle-mode`** (toggled alongside `.battle-active`) overrides the base `body`'s `padding: 8px` (`40px` on the bottom) and `min-height: 100vh` down to `padding: 0; min-height: 100dvh; overflow: hidden;` — both of the overridden properties would otherwise add on top of `#battle-scene`'s own 100dvh (the padding directly, the taller `100vh` floor indirectly) and push the page past one screen. Deployment keeps the original padding, since `body` never gets `.battle-mode` outside of battle.
- **Content inside each fixed-height band scales to fill it, rather than the band being sized by content**: `#battlefield`/`.bf-column` get `height:100%` under `.battle-active`; every direct `.card` child of a `.bf-column` (Ranged's fixed 5 slots whether filled or an invisible placeholder, and both enemy columns) gets `flex:1 1 0; min-height:0`, dividing the column's real height evenly — so a smaller team's heroes render proportionally larger instead of leaving dead space below a content-sized stack, and a full 5v5 team never pushes the row taller. `.hero-sprite` switched from a fixed `aspect-ratio:1/1; max-height:40px` (the old per-element trim) to `flex:1 1 auto; min-height:0`, so it fills whatever vertical space is left in the card after the header row/HP bar/mana bar/coverage tag take their own small natural heights — `background-size:contain` still keeps the art itself uncropped and undistorted regardless of the resulting box's aspect ratio (a non-square box just letterboxes on whichever axis has spare room, rather than the old fixed-square-then-shrink approach). `#board`'s `grid-template-rows` changed from a fixed `repeat(5, 44px)` to `repeat(5, 1fr)`, so the 5 tile rows divide the `1fr` "remainder" band evenly — tiles can end up slightly non-square on some screen ratios as a result, an accepted tradeoff since filling the remainder band takes priority over a fixed tile aspect per the request.
- **Real bug caught by direct measurement**: player Melee cards are absolutely positioned (needed for `positionMeleeCardPairs()`'s band-alignment math) and therefore don't participate in `.bf-column`'s flex sizing at all — without an explicit height, a Melee card's `.hero-sprite` (now `flex:1`) would have nothing to flex against and collapse toward zero. Fixed in `renderBattlefield()` by measuring a real rendered Ranged slot's height right after the Ranged column is appended to the DOM (`rangedCol.querySelector('.card').getBoundingClientRect().height`) and setting that explicitly via `card.style.height` on every Melee hero card. The first attempt measured `rangedCol.firstElementChild` instead of querying for `.card` specifically, and accidentally grabbed the column's own `.bf-column-label` text node (~15px) rather than an actual slot card (~74px) — a column's real first child is its label, appended by `makeColumnEl()` before any slot cards. Caught immediately by comparing measured `meleeCardHeights` against `rangedCardHeight` via Playwright rather than eyeballing a screenshot.
- **`window.scrollTo(0, 0)` still runs at the end of `startBattle()`, `restartBattle()`, and `goToDeployment()`.** Without it, a screen transition inherits whatever scroll position the previous screen was left at — concretely, tapping "Start Battle" (which can sit well below the fold after scrolling through the roster) left the battle screen scrolled partway down, so its own top (the first Ranged row) was already off-screen even though the fixed-height layout itself now fits in one viewport.
- **A real, recurring bug pattern was already documented on `#battle-scene`/`#battle-controls-row`**: `body` is a flex column with `align-items:center`, so a flex child with no declared `width` of its own shrink-wraps to its children's natural content size instead of the available viewport width. Both elements already carry the required explicit `width:100%; max-width:420px;` from an earlier round — worth checking for on any new top-level wrapper added under `body` (see DECISIONS.md).
- **Mana shard particles are unaffected by `#battle-scene`'s new `overflow:hidden`** (added as a safety net so nothing in the fixed grid can ever grow past its band): `shardLayer` — the element both the player's tile-to-mana-bar shards and the enemy's off-screen-falling shards fly through — is appended directly to `document.body`, not as a descendant of `#battle-scene`, and was already architected to fly "independent of either element's own stacking/overflow context" per its own code comment. Confirmed via a full turn-cycle test (End Turn → enemy turn resolving → back to player) that the page stays pinned at exactly `100dvh` throughout, with zero layout breakage from attack animations or pose swaps.
- **"Restart Battle" (labeled "Restart") and "Change Deployment" (labeled "Leave") sit in `#battle-controls-row`, to the right of End Turn** — grouped in `#battle-leave-group` and absolutely positioned `right:0`, mirroring how `#btn-auto-attack` is anchored `left:0` — so End Turn stays pixel-centered regardless of either side's width. Both buttons briefly shared the identical label "Leave" in an earlier round; a direct follow-up request ("change the 'Leave' button that's supposed to restart the battle to 'Restart'") split `#btn-restart-battle` back out to its own distinct "Restart" label, while `#btn-redeploy` kept "Leave" — each button's `title` tooltip still carries its full original meaning regardless of visible label.

## Auto Attack Toggle
- `#btn-auto-attack` sits far-left of End Turn in the same row (`#battle-controls-row`, absolutely positioned so End Turn stays pixel-centered on the row regardless of Auto Attack's own width, which changes between its "OFF"/"ON" labels — a flex/grid balancing trick would need to react to that dynamically, absolute positioning doesn't need to). Visible only during battle, same as End Turn.
- It's a standing preference (`autoAttackEnabled`), not per-battle state — toggling it doesn't get reset by `startBattle()`/`restartBattle()` the way e.g. `swapUsedThisTurn` does, so it stays on (or off) across a redeploy or restart until the player changes it again.
- When on: after the player's own swap resolves (the Match-3 board itself is always fully manual — auto-attack never touches it), each living player hero that can afford its Basic Attack auto-resolves one at a time, paced via `scheduleAutoAttack()`'s `AUTO_ATTACK_DELAY_MS` (500ms) so the player can follow what's happening rather than everything resolving in one instant. Target selection reuses `getValidTargets()` and picks randomly among them — the exact same target-picking rule the enemy AI (`enemyTurn()`) already uses, just applied to the player's side and phrased as "semi-logical" rather than tactical.
- A Hero (`isSpecial`) that can already afford its Special always uses it (no reason to hold back an attack it can already use). One that can afford Basic but not yet Special gets a random `AUTO_ATTACK_BANK_CHANCE` (40%) chance to bank mana instead — "saving for a greater attack" — functionally identical to manually picking Do Nothing. Normal characters have no such option and always attack if able, same rule as manual play (see `normalCharacterMustActFirst()`).
- Implementation detail: `runNextAutoAttack()` resolves exactly one hero, then relies on `performAttack()`'s own existing call to `checkAutoEndPlayerTurn()` (or, for the no-op "bank"/"no valid target" branches, a direct call) to continue the chain — `checkAutoEndPlayerTurn()` calls `scheduleAutoAttack()` again if heroes remain, or ends the turn via the existing `allResolved` path once everyone's resolved. No separate auto-attack-specific turn-ending logic was needed.

- **Runic Wave with multiple Melee stacked on the same lane**: `resolveRunicWaveHits()` picks exactly one guard per lane via `heroes.find(...)` (first match in heroes/placement order), per lane, independently. Tested case: enemy had Sylra (aligned lane 0), Kael (boundary lanes 0/1), and Korr (aligned lane 1) all alive. The wave hit Sylra (lane 0's first-found guard) and Kael (lane 1's first-found guard, since Kael precedes Korr in the heroes array) — Korr took no damage and had no effect on exposure at all, despite also guarding lane 1.
