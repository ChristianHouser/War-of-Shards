## Core Rules You Must Never Violate
- Match-3 board is on the **bottom**. Its baseline purpose is generating mana/stamina, but matches of 4+ tiles now spawn special tiles (Arcane Bomb, Runic Crystal) that CAN deal direct damage to the enemy team when later matched or activated — see "Match-3 Match Type Rules & Special Tiles" below and context/GAME_MECHANICS.md for the full mechanic. Match-3 still never creates new soldier/hero units on the battlefield — only damage and mana, never new combatants.
- Battlefield is on the **upper half** with left-vs-right positioning.
- Each side has a Melee zone (front) and Ranged zone (back). Team size is capped at `TEAM_CAP = 5` characters per side, any mix of Ranged/Melee. Both zones are placed by drag-and-drop: Ranged always shows `RANGED_SLOTS` (= 5) fixed, always-visible slots and the player drags a hero onto whichever empty slot they choose (gaps allowed). Melee has no slots of its own, but every Melee card visually snaps onto the Ranged grid: level with one Ranged row (`band: {type:'aligned', index}`), or straddling the half-step gap between two adjacent rows (`band: {type:'boundary', indices:[i,j]}`) — always resolvable since the Ranged grid exists whether filled or not. Two Melee may not share the same `band` (one guard per row/half-step; a drop onto an already-occupied band is rejected). What it actually protects (`coverage`) is derived live from `band` + which Ranged slots are currently filled, not frozen at drop time — see context/GAME_MECHANICS.md for the exact mechanism.
- Melee units can always attack enemy Melee. Melee can attack a specific enemy Ranged unit only if no living enemy Melee's `coverage` currently guards that unit's lane — this is a **per-lane** check, not an all-or-nothing "whole zone must be cleared" rule.
- Ranged units can attack any enemy.
- Every hero has their **own individual mana/stamina bar**.
- Battlefield capacity per side is `TEAM_CAP = 5` characters total (Ranged + Melee combined, any mix). The "Max 6v6" framing still applies at the campaign/meta level; this screen's capacity supersedes it locally.
- Heroes are drawn from a fixed named roster (see characters.js / Roster.html — currently 45 characters), not randomly generated. The player may deploy any of them; the enemy's team does not restrict the player's picks.
- The **enemy team is randomized fresh every time deployment is (re)entered** (`randomizeEnemyTeam()`, called from `initDeployment()`) — it is not fixed/static. Always `TEAM_CAP` (5) total, but the Ranged/Melee split itself is also randomized: one of two `ENEMY_FORMATIONS` is picked with equal probability each time — 3 Melee/2 Ranged (one Melee aligned per lane, one straddling the boundary between them) or 2 Melee/3 Ranged (two boundary guards side by side, covering all 3 lanes between them). Two hard constraints on every draw regardless of formation: at most 2 of the 5 may be Heroes (isSpecial), and the 5 must show at least 4 distinct colors (out of the 5 possible). The hero cap is enforced by construction (decide the hero/non-hero split before picking characters, not by rejecting random draws); the color rule is enforced by retrying the draw.
- **The enemy's setup is hidden during deployment** — the deployment (character placement) screen shows no enemy section at all: only the player's own Ranged/Melee columns, centered in the available width. Neither the enemy's characters nor its formation (and therefore not the Ranged/Melee split) are visible before the player commits their own team. `ENEMY_TEAM` is still decided at deployment-entry time (not re-randomized at Start Battle) — only its on-screen reveal is deferred; the live battle screen (`renderBattlefield()`) shows the real roster (and both sides' "YOU"/"ENEMY" column labels) as normal.
- Basic attacks are cheap; unlocked abilities cost more mana/stamina. Most characters share the same Basic Attack cost/damage (`BASIC_COST`/`BASIC_DMG`), but a character may override this with its own `basicCost`/`basicDmg` fields in characters.js for a heavier weapon (higher cost, higher damage) — `basicCostFor()`/`basicDmgFor()` resolve the actual values, and `maxManaFor()` accounts for the override so the character can actually reach the mana their attack needs.
- **Manual attack input is "tap glowing hero → attack the Logical Target", with aiming always optional** (`onHeroClick()`, `resolveAttack()`): a player hero that can currently act "glows" (`.card.can-act`, gold pulse) — recomputed for every living hero (not just whichever ones an action directly touched) any time `checkAutoEndPlayerTurn()` runs, so it never goes stale for a hero whose own mana/color wasn't involved in the triggering action. Tapping it attacks immediately — a normal character attacks directly with Basic; a Hero (`isSpecial`) still opens its Basic/Special/Do Nothing popup first, and the attack resolves the instant Basic or Special is chosen. Tapping an enemy never itself attacks — it only sets/clears an optional pre-aim (`aimedTarget`, tap the same enemy again to clear); tapping a hero afterward attacks that pre-aimed enemy instead of the Logical Target, as long as it's still a legal target for that specific attacker (falls back to the Logical Target otherwise). Aim is single-use, consumed by the next attack regardless of which path resolved it. **Logical Targeting** (`pickLogicalTarget()`) ranks only the legal targets (`getValidTargets()` — the existing melee-can't-reach-guarded-ranged rule, never raw pixel/visual distance) by: 1) highest current mana (closest to affording Special, since `SPECIAL_COST` is the same flat threshold for everyone), 2) then lowest HP, 3) then nearest lane to the attacker's own (`heroLane()`, unifying Ranged's `laneIndex` and Melee's `primaryLane(coverage)`). This is separate from the Auto Attack toggle below, which still picks a uniformly random valid target. A Hero's mana bar additionally gets its own gold pulse (`.mana-bar.mana-full`) once its mana is completely full — Heroes only, since normal characters have no Special to signal readiness for.
- There is an optional Auto Attack toggle for the battlefield only — the Match-3 board always stays fully manual (the player still does every swap themselves). When on, each living player hero that can afford its Basic auto-attacks a random valid target (same target-picking rule the enemy AI already uses) once the player's swap resolves; a Hero (`isSpecial`) that can't yet afford its Special has a random chance to bank mana instead of attacking, to save toward a bigger attack later — a Hero that already can afford Special always uses it. Normal characters have no such option and always attack, same as manual play.
- **The deployment screen's battlefield layout and the live battle screen's battlefield layout must stay visually identical** for the player's own columns: Ranged is always the fixed `RANGED_SLOTS` grid (a hero's row never shifts based on how many other slots are filled), and Melee cards are always pixel-aligned to their `band` (the Ranged row/gap they were snapped to), not sorted into a plain list. This was a real bug once (battle used a compacted list, deployment used the fixed grid) — see DECISIONS.md.
- **The Formations button opens a swipeable picker of 6 preset unit-placement layouts** (`FORMATION_PRESETS`), not an explainer — the melee-coverage explainer text that used to live in that popup now lives in a small speech-bubble tooltip (`#formation-info-bubble`) toggled by a separate "ℹ" info button just left of the Formations button (`positionFormationInfoBubble()` keeps it on-screen). Each preset defines a Ranged-row/Melee-band shape (`ranged: [rowIndices]`, `melee: [{type, index|indices}]`, all pre-verified conflict-free via the same `bandsConflict()` rule real placement uses) and is shown as one carousel slide (name, one-line tactical blurb, mini diagram) — swipe or use ‹›/dots to browse, then "Apply This Formation" to use it. `applyFormationPreset()` clears the current deployment, then reassigns the player's *already-chosen* heroes (any position, either type) into the preset's shape first, only pulling fresh heroes from the roster to fill any remaining slots the player didn't have enough of — so applying a preset reorganizes an existing pick rather than discarding it.
- **Match-3 Match Type Rules & Special Tiles** (full detail in context/GAME_MECHANICS.md):
  - **Match of 3** — removes the tiles, grants 1 mana/tile to every living player hero of that color, no other effect.
  - **Match of 4** — same 1 mana/tile, PLUS spawns an **Arcane Bomb** on the board (`boardSpecial[y][x] = 'bomb'`). When later matched or activated (matched into a new group — i.e. the player swaps in at least two more same-color tiles that connect to it — OR the player swaps any adjacent tile into/out of it even without forming a new match — OR the player directly taps its tile, no swap needed), it clears itself + its 4 orthogonal neighbors, all 5 cells pay mana by their own color, and it deals 2 damage to one random living enemy Melee (front-line) unit.
  - **Match of 5+ (straight run, or a T/L/plus formed by two intersecting runs of ≥3)** — same 1 mana/tile, PLUS spawns a **Runic Crystal** (`boardSpecial[y][x] = 'crystal'`). A crystal activates two ways: the player **directly taps its tile** (a single click, no swap needed — same as before), OR it gets **matched into a new group** the same way a Bomb does (the player swaps in at least two more same-color tiles that connect to it into a fresh ≥3 group) — either path opens the same choice modal. Being merely touched by an unrelated (non-matching, non-connecting) swap still does nothing special. A tap (or an auto-trigger via match) counts as the turn's one board action, same as a swap. Either way, the crystal's own tile always clears + pays its base 1 mana first, then the player chooses: Option A clears every tile of that color on the board (mana for all of them), or Option B ("Runic Wave") deals damage across all `RANGED_SLOTS` rows simultaneously instead (see `resolveRunicWaveHits()` — full-cover melee blocks the ranged unit behind it entirely; a straddling/boundary melee takes the full 3 once and one of its two guarded ranged lanes takes 2, the other stays protected).
  - There is no separate "+1 bonus mana" for 5-tile matches anymore — differentiation between tiers is entirely about which special tile spawns, not extra mana.
  - **Tile swaps visually slide, they never teleport.** `animateTileSwap()` swaps the two tiles' board data, then FLIP-animates both tile elements sliding from their old grid position to their new one (`SWAP_SLIDE_MS = 160`ms) before match detection runs; a swap that produces no match (and activates no bomb) slides back the same way to visually revert. This applies to every swap-driven path, not just player input.
  - **Every tile cleared by any path — plain match, bomb blast, or crystal clear — spawns tiny glittering shard particles that fly toward the mana bar of each living player hero who shares that tile's color**, as a visual echo of the mana that hero is about to receive (`spawnManaShards()`, hooked into `processMatches()`'s single cell-clearing commit point). Purely cosmetic — it has no effect on mana math, which is still computed separately.
- **Live battle HP is shown as a depleting bar with the numeric `hp/maxHp` ratio overlaid on top** (`.hp-bar-bg`/`.hp-bar`/`.hp-num`, built in `buildHeroCardEl()`, updated in `updateHeroUI()`) — this applies to the live battle screen only. The deployment screen's preview card still shows plain `N HP` text (`.hp-text`) since it only ever displays full, undamaged HP.
- **There is a deliberate ~900ms pause (`ENEMY_TURN_START_DELAY_MS`) between the player's turn ending and the enemy actually acting** — the "Enemy Turn..." indicator and board lock appear instantly, but the enemy's mana boost and attacks wait a beat first, so the handoff reads as a real turn change rather than an instant cut.
- **The enemy's per-turn mana boost (`enemyTurn()`'s "simulates their match" gain) shows shard particles falling from off-screen above the viewport into the boosted hero's mana bar** (`spawnEnemyManaShards()`), since the enemy has no visible match-3 board of their own to spawn shards from a tile — as if their board is hidden just above the screen. Shares the same particle-flight mechanics as the player's tile-to-mana-bar shards (`flyManaShards()`), just with a different, off-screen start point.
- **Battle art assets live under `/Artwork`** (added directly to the repo, not generated) — real pixel-art battlefield backgrounds and character pose sets, deliberately far fewer than the 45 named roster characters (only 3 Ranged skins, 4 Melee skins). `#battlefield` picks one of 5 map backgrounds (3 day, 2 night) uniformly at random every time a battle starts (`pickRandomMapBackground()`, called from `startBattle()`/`restartBattle()`) — plain solid color during deployment, before any battle has picked one. `Day Forest Map3.png` (a meadow running down into visible dirt/tree-roots at the bottom of the same canvas) was added directly by the user as a 5th background, at the same 941×1672 portrait canvas size as the other "Map" variants — it needed no code changes beyond one new line in `MAP_BACKGROUNDS`, since its composition already puts the "underground" visual right where `background-position:top center` + the shared `#battle-scene` wrapper naturally lands it behind the Match-3 board. Every deployed hero (both sides) is assigned one of the available skins by `assignHeroArt()` (called at the end of `buildArmies()`): grouped by side+type, in deployment order, cycling back to the start of that type's pool (`% pool.length`) once a side has more of that type than the pool has entries — i.e. "use a copy of that type" once you exceed 3 Ranged or 4 Melee on one side.
- **Live battle hero cards show the sprite fully visible, card-chrome-free, with HP/mana below it** (`.card.sprite-style`, per direct request) — no background box, no border, no fixed height on the card itself; a `.hero-sprite` child (a plain square, `background-size:contain` so the art is never cropped) is the whole visual, with a `.card-header-row` (small inline color dot + name) above it and the HP/mana bars stacked below, all in normal flex-column flow rather than overlaid on top of the art. `heroSpriteUrl()` sets each pose's URL via the `.style.backgroundImage` **DOM property**, not inlined into an HTML string — an inline `style="background-image:url(...)"` string would nest double-quotes inside the attribute's own double-quotes and get silently truncated by the HTML parser, a real bug hit here (see DECISIONS.md). `triggerHeroPose(hero, 'attack'|'impacted')` briefly swaps the sprite (not the card) to the Attack pose (on the attacker, hooked into `performAttack()`) or Impacted pose (on the target, hooked into `performAttack()` and `dealBoardDamage()`) for `HERO_POSE_DURATION_MS` (550ms) before reverting to Idle — fire-and-forget, same spirit as the mana shard particles. An unfilled Ranged slot during battle (`buildEmptySpriteSlot()`) mirrors the exact same name/sprite/bar structure (just invisible) so its height still matches a filled row's at any viewport width — Melee band alignment depends on every Ranged row's live rect being consistent, filled or not. Deployment preview cards are unaffected — they still use the old boxed `.card` look (this change is battle-only). `.card` also sets `-webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent` — without it, mobile browsers flash a translucent grey rectangle over the whole tapped card on every tap, which reads exactly like a leftover "card box" around an otherwise chrome-free sprite even though nothing in the stylesheet draws one.
- **The battle map background spans #battlefield AND the Match-3 board as one continuous image**, not just the battlefield — a shared `#battle-scene` wrapper (around both, see the HTML) carries the picture (`pickRandomMapBackground()`), while `#battlefield` and `#board-wrap` clear their own fills to `transparent` so it shows through both. The point, per direct instruction, is that a map's lower reaches (a stone/tiled "floor" in the art) land behind the Match-3 grid, reading as "the match-3 section is underground." The image itself carries **no dark tint overlay** — per direct feedback the art should show "vibrant, invisible as the original work," so `pickRandomMapBackground()` sets the plain `url(...)` with nothing layered over it.
- **During battle, the screen is a fixed viewport-percentage layout, not natural content-flow sized to fit**, per direct request: the top ~9% is scenery, the next 50% is the battlefield, a controls row takes only what it needs, Match-3 absorbs whatever's left, and the final ~4% is a reserved empty bottom buffer (phone case/bezel clearance). Implemented as `#battle-scene.battle-active { display:grid; grid-template-rows: 9% 50% auto 1fr 4%; height:100dvh; }` (`100dvh`, not `100vh`, since `100vh` can exceed what's actually visible while a mobile browser's address bar is still showing) — percentages resolve against the grid container's own height, and `1fr` automatically claims "the remainder" for Match-3 with no manual math. Two structural, content-free divs (`#scenery-band`, `#bottom-buffer`) fill the fixed rows that have no real UI of their own; the shared map background (see below) shows through both since neither has its own fill. `body.battle-mode` (toggled alongside `battle-active`) zeroes the base `body`'s padding and caps `min-height` to `100dvh` — both would otherwise stack on top of `#battle-scene`'s own 100dvh and push the page past one screen. Content inside the fixed bands scales to fill them rather than driving their size: `.bf-column`'s cards (Ranged's fixed 5 slots, both enemy columns) get `flex:1 1 0` so fewer heroes render proportionally larger instead of leaving dead space, `.hero-sprite` is `flex:1 1 auto` (not a fixed `aspect-ratio`/`max-height`) so it fills whatever room is left after the header/HP/mana rows, and `#board`'s tile rows are `1fr` instead of a fixed px height. Player Melee cards are absolutely positioned (for band-alignment) and don't participate in that flex sizing at all — `renderBattlefield()` gives them an explicit pixel height instead, measured from a real rendered Ranged slot's `getBoundingClientRect()`. `#turn-indicator` lives inside `#end-turn-wrap` (not as a standalone sibling after `#battle-scene`) specifically so it rides inside the `auto` controls row instead of adding a 6th row's worth of height below the whole 100dvh grid — though per direct request it's now left permanently hidden (never toggled visible), so today it contributes no height there at all. Deployment never gets the `battle-active`/`battle-mode` classes, so its natural-flow, scrollable layout (and the older per-property trims like `.hero-sprite`'s previous fixed `max-height`, now superseded) is completely unaffected. `startBattle()`/`restartBattle()`/`goToDeployment()` all still call `window.scrollTo(0, 0)` — without it, a screen transition can inherit whatever scroll position the previous screen was left at. Both `#battle-controls-row` and `#battle-scene` must always set their own explicit `width:100%; max-width:420px` — a flex child of `body` (which uses `align-items:center`) with no declared width of its own shrink-wraps to its children's content size instead of the available viewport width, a recurring bug pattern in this file (see DECISIONS.md) worth checking for on any new top-level wrapper added under `body`.
- **"Restart Battle" and "Change Deployment" (both now labeled "Leave", distinguished only by a `title` tooltip) sit in `#battle-leave-group`, to the right of End Turn** in the same row as Auto Attack — absolutely positioned (`right:0`) mirroring Auto Attack's `left:0` anchor, so End Turn stays centered regardless of either side's width. They used to live in their own row below the Match-3 board.
  - Match-3 grouping uses connected-component flood fill (`findMatchGroups()`), not flat per-color totals, so an intersecting horizontal+vertical run registers as one shaped group.

## How to Work
- After finishing a task, update TODO.md with what was done and what the next AI should do.
- When you make a design decision, record it in context/DECISIONS.md.
- Keep all new design documents inside the /context/ folder.
- Prefer clear, modular Markdown files over long walls of text.
- If something is ambiguous, list it under “Open Design Questions” instead of inventing a permanent rule.

## Project Goals
- Mobile-first (must run well on most phones and tablets)
- Fantasy theme
- Hero collection + Campaign + PvP + Hero side-story missions

Stay consistent with the existing design. Do not redesign core systems unless explicitly asked.
